# Blog Post 4: housing prices, rates, and the payment burden

**Question:** How did the income burden of financing a new U.S. home change
between 2019 and 2024, and what does the interest rate contribute?

The complete post uses base R plus **ggplot2**. The analysis has no custom
functions, API keys, microdata, or inflation-factor lookups. Windows users
can use an existing ggplot2 installation or its binary package without
compiling source code. All numeric statements are generated by inline R expressions.
The supplied CSVs let the post render without downloading anything.

## Optional: refresh the raw data programmatically

From the website root:

```r
post_dir <- "blog/posts/post4"
source(file.path(post_dir, "R", "download_data.R"))
```

This uses `download.file` to call FRED's public CSV URLs, with no API key or
account. It validates each temporary download before replacing that CSV and
records the retrieval date in `data/raw/source_manifest.csv`. The analysis
window stays fixed at 2019–2024. Live sources can revise historical values;
keep the bundled snapshots for exact replication of the supplied figures.
If refreshing fails due to an SSL or network error, keep the included CSVs
and render normally. No refresh is necessary for submitting the post.

## Sources and units

Raw download window: **January 1, 2015–December 31, 2024**. The R analysis uses
only **2019–2024**. Snapshots acquired programmatically on **October 5, 2026**.

| FRED ID | Original producer | Definition and units | Original frequency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [MSPUS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MSPUS) | Census Bureau and HUD | Median sale price of **new** U.S. houses; current dollars, not seasonally adjusted | Quarterly |
| [MORTGAGE30US](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MORTGAGE30US) | Freddie Mac | 30-year fixed mortgage interest rate; percent, not seasonally adjusted | Weekly, ending Thursday |
| [MEHOINUSA646N](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MEHOINUSA646N) | Census Bureau | Median household income; current dollars, not seasonally adjusted | Annual |

These published aggregate datasets are new to this analysis; no IPUMS extract
is used. Freddie Mac's series is copyrighted with citation required; its
source and attribution appear in the article and the mortgage-rate figure.
The other two series are tagged public domain, citation requested, by FRED.
Freddie Mac changed survey methodology on November 17, 2022.

## Methods and interpretation

- Price proxy = arithmetic mean of four quarterly medians. It is **not** an
  annual median or a constant-quality house-price index. Changes in the mix of
  new homes sold can move this proxy.
- Rate = arithmetic mean of 52 or 53 weekly observations per calendar year.
  Income = the published annual household median. No 2026 partial-year input
  or unsupported inflation factor enters the calculation.
- Loan = 80% of the price proxy; term = 360 months. Monthly principal and
  interest are `loan * m / (1 - (1 + m)^(-360))`, with `m = rate_pct / 1200`.
  The payment at average inputs is not an average of actual borrowers' payments.
- Payment burden = `100 * monthly_payment / (annual_income / 12)`.
  A common CPI adjustment cancels in this same-year ratio. The indexed price
  and income chart uses nominal growth with a shared base year, and is explicitly
  labeled as such; it does not claim to show real wealth or real income growth.
- Fixed-rate scenario uses the 2019 annual-average mortgage rate in every year,
  retaining observed price and income. This is a mechanical comparison, not a
  causal prediction of what housing prices would be under different rates.
- All-household income and new-home transaction prices describe different
  populations. Their ratio is an illustrative national benchmark, not an
  estimate of the median actual buyer's debt-to-income ratio.
- The 20% down payment is held constant. Taxes, insurance, PMI, fees,
  maintenance, and liquidity constraints are omitted. Old fixed-rate owners
  need not face the rate applying to a new mortgage.
